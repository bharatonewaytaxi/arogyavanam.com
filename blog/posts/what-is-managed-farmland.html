If you live in Bengaluru and have thought about owning a piece of land outside the city, you have probably heard the term managed farmland. It sounds simple, but it covers a wide range of projects, promises and price points. This guide explains what managed farmland actually is, what the developer is responsible for, what you as the owner receive, which costs to expect, and how to judge a project before you put money into it.

We have written it in plain language for families who want land they can visit, enjoy and hold for the long term, but who do not have the time to farm it themselves.

What is managed farmland?

Managed farmland is agricultural land that you own outright, while a developer or management company handles the farming, upkeep and security for you. You buy a defined plot, it is registered in your name, and a professional team plants it, irrigates it, protects it and maintains it on your behalf.

The idea is to separate ownership from day-to-day work. A family in Whitefield or Hebbal can own farmland near Bagepalli without needing to hire labour, arrange water, deal with fencing or travel every week. The management team does that work under an agreement, and the owner can visit whenever they like.

Managed farmland is different from a plain plotted layout, where you buy land and are left to develop it yourself, and different from a pure plantation scheme, where you may only hold a share of the produce and not the land. In a well-structured managed farmland project, you hold both: the land title and a defined share of what the land produces.

What the developer does

The value of managed farmland comes almost entirely from the quality of the management. A good developer typically takes care of four areas.

Plantation

The developer chooses crops that suit the soil, rainfall and climate, then plants and tends them. At Arogya Vana, the plantation is agronomist-run and includes white sandalwood, casuarina, and seasonal fruits and vegetables. Long-cycle trees like sandalwood need years of steady care, which is exactly where professional management matters.

Irrigation and water

Water is the single biggest factor in whether a farm thrives. Look for drip irrigation, a reliable water source and some form of rainwater harvesting. Arogya Vana uses drip irrigation, water management and rainwater harvesting across the plantation.

Security

An unattended farm is vulnerable to trespass, grazing animals and theft, especially when it holds valuable trees. A managed project should provide round-the-clock protection. At Arogya Vana this means 24/7 security, CCTV and solar fencing.

Legal and paperwork

Buying agricultural land involves title checks and several registrations. Some developers leave all of this to the buyer; others handle it in-house. Arogya Vana handles title verification, registration, EC, mutation and the sale deed in-house, so families are guided through each step.

What the owner gets

As an owner of managed farmland, you should expect three things to be spelled out clearly in writing.

  • Ownership of the land. The plot should be registered in your name, with 100% ownership, not a lease or a share certificate.
  • A defined share of farm income. The agreement should say exactly how crop income is split. At Arogya Vana, 70% of sandalwood profit and 100% of seasonal fruit and vegetable income go to the owner, subject to the terms of the agreement.
  • Access and enjoyment. Many families buy managed farmland as much for weekends and fresh air as for the land itself. Arogya Vana includes resort-style amenities: a clubhouse and cottages, pool, yoga deck, gym, play area and jogging trail. Site visits run every day except Tuesday, 9 AM to 6 PM.

Be careful with any project that promises fixed or guaranteed returns. Farm income depends on crop yields, market prices, weather and management performance, and land values move with the wider market. A responsible developer will describe returns as being as per the agreement, with terms that you can read and check.

Costs to expect

Before you buy, list every cost you are likely to pay over the years you hold the land, not only the headline price.

Purchase price

This is the price of the plot and, in managed projects, often a separate managed investment that funds the plantation and upkeep. At Arogya Vana, a 4,400 sq.ft plot is offered on the ₹25 lakh plan: ₹15 lakh for the land plus ₹10 lakh managed investment, with returns as per the agreement and terms applying. Registration and legal charges

Stamp duty, registration fees and documentation costs are paid when the plot is registered in your name. Ask the developer for an estimate of these at the start so there are no surprises.

Maintenance after the free period

Most managed farmland projects charge an annual maintenance fee for ongoing farming and security. Some offer a free period first. At Arogya Vana, maintenance is free for the first 2 years, and charges apply from year 3. Ask how the fee is calculated and what it covers.

Your own travel and time

Even though the farm is managed, you will want to visit. Factor in the distance. Arogya Vana is near Bagepalli on the Karnataka–Andhra Pradesh border, about 2 hours from Bengaluru and 15 minutes from NH44.

How to judge a managed farmland project

Not every project labelled "managed farmland" is equal. Use this five-point checklist before you commit.

1. Title

Confirm that the developer has a clear title to the land and that it can be legally transferred to you. Ask for copies of the title documents and have them reviewed by an independent lawyer.

2. Encumbrance certificate (EC)

The EC shows whether the land has any registered loans, mortgages or disputes. Check that it is clean for a long enough period.

3. The agreement

Read the management agreement line by line. It should state what the developer will do, how income is shared, what maintenance costs apply and when, and what happens if either side wants to exit. Avoid projects that rely on verbal promises.

4. Track record

Look at what the developer has already delivered. Arogya Vana is a project of Saptha Ventures, which has a track record of plotted developments. Phase 1 (5 acres, 2,000+ trees) is sold out, Phase 2 (13 acres) is about 75% sold, and Phase 3 is now open.

5. A site visit

Nothing replaces walking the land. See the plantation, the irrigation, the fencing and the amenities for yourself, and talk to the farm team. You can book a site visit any day except Tuesday.

Is managed farmland right for you?

Managed farmland can suit families who want to own land near Bengaluru, enjoy it on weekends, and hold it for the long term while professionals take care of the farming. It is less suitable for anyone who needs quick access to their money, since land is not as easy to sell as shares or gold, and it is not suitable for anyone looking for guaranteed returns.

If it fits your goals, start by shortlisting projects, checking the documents, and visiting in person. Then compare the agreements side by side.
